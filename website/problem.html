The problem

Maintenance here is still mostly a guess.

Cement plants run a continuous chain of crushers, mills, and kilns under extreme heat, dust, and load. When one fails without warning, the cost is measured in days, not hours.

Two strategies, both flawed

Reactive maintenance waits for something to break, then absorbs the full cost of unplanned downtime — and sourcing parts or a specialist to a remote plant can add days on its own. Calendar-based preventive maintenance avoids some of that risk, but it replaces healthy parts on a fixed schedule and still misses damage that develops faster than the calendar expects.

What plant managers actually want

A 2025 analytical hierarchy process (AHP) study of Nigerian cement facilities asked plant managers to rank maintenance strategies directly. Predictive maintenance won by a wide margin.

StrategyScore
Predictive41.76%
Preventive31.66%
Corrective26.68%
Local context

What makes this harder in a Nigerian plant specifically.

Remote sites

Sourcing a specialist part or a diagnostic engineer to a rural plant can add days to a repair that would take hours somewhere better connected.

Grid reliability

Erratic power adds its own stress to motors and drives, on top of the thermal and mechanical load the equipment already carries.

Environment

Fine limestone dust and ambient heat above 40°C push both machinery and electronics harder than a temperate-climate plant.

Where this leaves us

Predictive maintenance is what plant managers say they want.

EchoWatch is what that looks like on real equipment, on a budget a university team can actually build.
