How it works

From vibration to a routed alert.

Four stages, running on a single ESP32 node per machine — from the moment a bearing starts to sound different to the moment the right person is told.

01

Sense

A MEMS microphone and accelerometer on the machine housing capture acoustic and vibration signal continuously, processed on-device.

02

Detect

A 1D convolutional autoencoder — trained only on that machine's healthy operation — flags rising reconstruction error as a deviation from normal.

03

Alert

Every reading and every anomaly score is logged to a time-series database, powering the live dashboard and a historical trend per machine.

04

Notify & escalate

A confirmed anomaly is routed to whoever is responsible for that specific machine. If the network is down and the alert can't be delivered, a local buzzer at the equipment fires instead — the alert never depends entirely on connectivity.

Per-equipment sensing

Six machines, six different failure signatures.

Crushers

Acoustic signature plus bearing vibration. A clean impact rhythm turns into a muffled drone as liners wear or a jam develops.

Raw Mills

Vibration and acoustic reading correlated against differential pressure, since an unstable grinding bed is the largest source of false alarms here.

Coal Mills

Vibration and motor current on the mill body. Gas safety monitoring stays on the plant's own certified system — never a demo sensor.

Kilns

A single infrared spot reading tracks shell temperature at one point per rotation, standing in for a full thermal-scanner array.

Cement Mills

The same acoustic principle used commercially for decades: grinding-media impact sound, mounted on the shell to stay clean of noise from neighboring machines.

Packing

Load-cell weight stability and motor current on the conveyors and rotary drives — the lightest-duty equipment on the line.
