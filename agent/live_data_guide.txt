LIVE DATA GUIDE – ADDITIONAL FAQ

Q: What is live data?
A: Live data refers to real-time measurements received from the IoT devices monitoring the machines.

Q: How often is live data updated?
A: The dashboard updates whenever new sensor data is transmitted and processed.

Q: What information is shown in live monitoring?
A: The system displays:

* Timestamp
* Horizontal acceleration
* Vertical acceleration
* RMS
* Kurtosis
* Anomaly score
* Energy deviation
* Asset Health Index (AHI)
* Machine status

Q: What does the timestamp represent?
A: The timestamp indicates when the sensor measurement was collected.

Q: Why are timestamps important?
A: They help users determine exactly when changes in machine behavior occurred.

Q: Why is live monitoring important?
A: Live monitoring enables early detection of abnormal machine conditions and supports timely maintenance decisions.

Q: What is horizontal acceleration?
A: Horizontal acceleration measures machine vibrations along the horizontal axis.

Q: What is vertical acceleration?
A: Vertical acceleration measures machine vibrations along the vertical axis.

Q: Why are two vibration directions monitored?
A: Different mechanical faults may appear more strongly in different directions.

Q: Why are acceleration values sometimes negative?
A: Acceleration is directional, so negative values simply indicate vibration in the opposite direction.

Q: What are the units of acceleration?
A: Acceleration is typically measured in g (gravitational acceleration).

Q: What does RMS mean in live monitoring?
A: RMS represents the overall vibration energy of the machine.

Q: Why does RMS change over time?
A: RMS changes as machine operating conditions, loads, and fault severity change.

Q: What is considered a high RMS value?
A: High RMS values depend on the machine type, but sudden increases often indicate abnormal conditions.

Q: What is kurtosis in live monitoring?
A: Kurtosis measures impulsive vibration events and can indicate bearing defects or repeated impacts.

Q: Why is kurtosis useful?
A: It helps identify faults that may not significantly affect overall vibration levels.

Q: What does a high kurtosis value mean?
A: High kurtosis often indicates localized defects, impacts, or bearing damage.

Q: What is the anomaly score?
A: The anomaly score measures how different current machine behavior is from normal operating conditions.

Q: Why is the anomaly score important?
A: It helps detect faults that may not be obvious from traditional vibration metrics.

Q: What does a high anomaly score mean?
A: A high anomaly score indicates that the machine is behaving abnormally.

Q: What is energy deviation?
A: Energy deviation measures changes in vibration energy under different loading conditions.

Q: Why is energy deviation monitored?
A: It helps account for changes caused by varying machine loads.

Q: What is AHI?
A: AHI (Asset Health Index) is a numerical estimate of machine health ranging from 0 to 1.

Q: Why is AHI important in live monitoring?
A: It provides an easy-to-understand summary of machine health.

Q: What does the machine status indicate?
A: The status indicates the current health category of the machine.

Q: What are the machine status categories?

Healthy:
AHI = 0.80–1.00

Warning:
AHI = 0.68–0.80

Degraded:
AHI = 0.20–0.68

Critical:
AHI ≤ 0.20

Q: Why is my machine status changing frequently?
A: Changes in load, vibration conditions, or developing faults can cause status changes.

Q: Why are graphs important?
A: Graphs help users identify trends and detect gradual deterioration.

Q: What live graphs are available?
A:

* AHI trend graph
* RMS trend graph
* Kurtosis trend graph
* Acceleration graphs
* Alert history graph

Q: Why should I monitor trends instead of single values?
A: Trends provide more reliable information about machine deterioration over time.

Q: What does a sudden spike in RMS indicate?
A: It may indicate sudden mechanical changes such as impacts, imbalance, or severe faults.

Q: What does a sudden spike in kurtosis indicate?
A: It often suggests impulsive events such as bearing defects or repeated impacts.

Q: Why are there occasional spikes in acceleration?
A: Temporary spikes may occur due to startup conditions, impacts, or environmental disturbances.

Q: Why does AHI sometimes fluctuate?
A: Minor fluctuations are normal due to changing machine conditions.

Q: Why does the dashboard smooth data?
A: Smoothing reduces noise and minimizes false alarms.

Q: What is a rolling average?
A: A rolling average computes the average of recent measurements to produce more stable indicators.

Q: Why is a 30-snapshot rolling window used?
A: It balances responsiveness with stability.

Q: What is lead time?
A: Lead time is the period between fault detection and actual machine failure.

Q: Why is lead time important?
A: It provides maintenance teams enough time to plan repairs before catastrophic failure occurs.

Q: What is Remaining Useful Life (RUL)?
A: RUL estimates how much operational life remains before failure.

Q: Does the current system estimate RUL?
A: The current version focuses on health estimation and anomaly detection, but future versions may include RUL prediction.

Q: Why are live values delayed?
A: Delays may result from network latency, backend processing, or communication issues.

Q: Why are there missing live data points?
A: Temporary network interruptions or device restarts may cause missing records.

Q: Why is no live data appearing?
A: Possible causes include:

* Device offline
* Sensor failure
* Backend server issues
* Network problems

Q: How can I determine if a machine is deteriorating?
A: Indicators include:

* Increasing RMS
* Increasing kurtosis
* Increasing anomaly score
* Decreasing AHI

Q: What should I do if AHI is continuously decreasing?
A: Schedule an inspection and monitor the machine more closely.

Q: What should I do if the machine enters Critical status?
A: Immediate inspection and corrective action are recommended.

Q: Can live data be exported?
A: Yes. Historical and live data can typically be exported through the dashboard.

Q: Can multiple users monitor live data simultaneously?
A: Yes. Multiple users can view live machine information concurrently.

Q: How long is historical live data stored?
A: Storage duration depends on system configuration and database policies.

Q: Why is machine comparison useful?
A: Comparing machines helps identify abnormal behavior relative to similar assets.

Q: Can environmental conditions affect live data?
A: Yes. Temperature, nearby machinery, and operating conditions can influence vibration measurements.

Q: Why is machine startup behavior different?
A: Machines often exhibit temporary vibration increases during startup and shutdown phases.

Q: Why is machine load information important?
A: Different loads can significantly affect vibration behavior and health indicators.

Q: How should operators use live data?
A:

1. Monitor trends.
2. Investigate unusual changes.
3. Respond to alerts promptly.
4. Schedule maintenance when degradation is detected.
5. Avoid relying solely on a single measurement.
