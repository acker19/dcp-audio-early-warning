IOT SENSOR SETUP GUIDE – ADDITIONAL FAQ

Q: What is the purpose of the IoT device?
A: The IoT device collects vibration data from machines, computes health indicators, and transmits information to the dashboard.

Q: What hardware components are required?
A: The system requires:

* ESP32 microcontroller
* Accelerometer or vibration sensor
* Power supply
* WiFi connection
* Mounting accessories

Q: Which microcontroller is used?
A: The prototype uses the ESP32 due to its processing capability and built-in WiFi support.

Q: What type of sensor is used?
A: The system uses an accelerometer or vibration sensor capable of measuring machine vibrations.

Q: Why are vibration sensors used?
A: Vibration changes often occur before mechanical failures become severe, making them useful for early fault detection.

Q: Where should the sensor be installed?
A: The sensor should be mounted as close as possible to the machine's bearings or vibration source.

Q: What are the best sensor locations?
A:

* Bearing housings
* Motor casing
* Fan supports
* Pump bearing assemblies
* Gearbox housing

Q: Where should the sensor NOT be installed?
A:

* Loose surfaces
* Plastic covers
* Moving components
* Areas with excessive external interference

Q: Why is sensor mounting important?
A: Improper mounting can introduce noise and inaccurate vibration measurements.

Q: How should the sensor be mounted?
A: The sensor should be tightly attached using screws, adhesive mounts, magnets, or industrial mounting brackets.

Q: Can I use adhesive tape?
A: Temporary testing may use tape, but permanent installations should use rigid mounting methods.

Q: Can one sensor monitor multiple machines?
A: Generally, one sensor should monitor one machine to ensure accurate diagnostics.

Q: Can multiple sensors be installed on one machine?
A: Yes. Multiple sensors can improve fault localization and monitoring accuracy.

Q: What orientation should the sensor have?
A: The sensor should be aligned to measure horizontal and vertical vibrations consistently.

Q: Why are horizontal and vertical measurements required?
A: Different faults may appear more strongly in different vibration directions.

Q: What is the sampling frequency?
A: The system uses a sampling frequency of approximately 25.6 kHz.

Q: Why is a high sampling frequency necessary?
A: Higher sampling frequencies capture high-frequency vibration signatures associated with early bearing faults.

Q: What is a snapshot?
A: A snapshot is a short window of collected vibration samples used for analysis.

Q: How long is one snapshot?
A: One snapshot typically lasts 0.1 seconds.

Q: How many samples are collected per snapshot?
A: Approximately 2560 samples.

Q: What data is transmitted to the dashboard?
A:

* Timestamp
* Horizontal acceleration
* Vertical acceleration
* RMS
* Kurtosis
* Anomaly score
* AHI
* Device status

Q: Does the IoT device perform local processing?
A: Yes. Some health calculations can be performed on the device before transmission.

Q: What communication methods are supported?
A:

* WiFi
* MQTT
* HTTP APIs
* Future support for LoRa or GSM

Q: Does the device need internet access?
A: Internet access is required for cloud synchronization and remote monitoring.

Q: What happens if WiFi is unavailable?
A: The device may temporarily store data locally and upload it when connectivity is restored.

Q: How is the device powered?
A: The device may use:

* USB power
* AC adapter
* Industrial power supply
* Battery backup

Q: What voltage is required?
A: The ESP32 generally operates at 3.3 V logic and is commonly powered through a 5 V input source.

Q: How can I verify that the sensor is working?
A: Observe live vibration readings on the dashboard or check serial monitor outputs.

Q: How do I know if the sensor is disconnected?
A: Symptoms may include:

* No incoming data
* Constant zero values
* Offline device status

Q: Why are my vibration values extremely high?
A: Possible causes include:

* Loose mounting
* Severe machine faults
* Incorrect calibration
* Sensor malfunction

Q: Why are vibration values always near zero?
A: Possible causes include:

* Sensor disconnection
* Wiring issues
* Incorrect sensor orientation
* Device malfunction

Q: How often should the sensor be calibrated?
A: Calibration should be performed periodically or after hardware modifications.

Q: Does temperature affect measurements?
A: Extreme temperatures may influence sensor performance and should be considered during deployment.

Q: Can environmental noise affect the readings?
A: Yes. Nearby machines and external vibrations may introduce measurement noise.

Q: Can the device operate continuously?
A: Yes. The system is designed for continuous condition monitoring.

Q: How much data does the system generate?
A: The amount depends on sampling frequency and transmission intervals.

Q: Is data stored locally?
A: Depending on configuration, data may be temporarily stored in flash memory or an SD card.

Q: What happens if power is lost?
A: Data transmission stops until power is restored. Buffered data may be uploaded afterward if local storage is enabled.

Q: How can I restart the device?
A: The device can be restarted through:

* Physical reset button
* Remote restart command
* Power cycling

Q: How do I update device firmware?
A: Firmware updates may be performed manually or through Over-The-Air (OTA) updates.

Q: Does the system support OTA updates?
A: Future versions may support secure remote firmware updates.

Q: How do I register a new IoT device?
A:

1. Power on the device.
2. Connect it to WiFi.
3. Obtain the device ID.
4. Register the ID on the dashboard.
5. Assign the device to a machine.

Q: What is the device ID?
A: The device ID uniquely identifies each monitoring node.

Q: Can devices be moved between machines?
A: Yes, but recalibration and reassignment are recommended.

Q: How many devices can the system support?
A: The architecture can support multiple devices depending on server resources and network capacity.

Q: How do I know if the device is online?
A: The dashboard will display an Online status and recent timestamps.

Q: What does Offline status mean?
A: The server has not received data from the device within the expected period.

Q: How secure is communication?
A: Communication may use authentication tokens, encrypted APIs, and secure network protocols.

Q: What maintenance does the IoT device require?
A:

* Inspect mounting periodically
* Check wiring connections
* Verify network connectivity
* Update firmware when necessary
* Recalibrate sensors periodically
